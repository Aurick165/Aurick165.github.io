1T1R ReRAM Characterization

How forming, SET, and RESET conditions shape the conductive filament in TaOx.

What this work is about

In a 1T1R cell, the access transistor is more than a selector — biased correctly, it becomes a fast, local current limiter that responds far quicker than an instrument's compliance loop. This work characterizes TaOx-based devices on a Keysight B1500 semiconductor parameter analyzer, a transistor-saturation-limited control, where a low word-line voltage during SET caps the filament's growth current and a high word-line voltage during RESET lets the cell draw what it needs to dissolve the filament.

Methods

Forming, quasi-static I–V sweeps, and pulsed SET/RESET cycling across multiple device samples; a Python measurement framework automates sweep sequences, SET-detection thresholds, and data logging, enabling systematic studies of memory window, switching variability, and endurance as a function of programming conditions.

Electrothermal Filament Modeling

Where the heat goes during resistive switching — finite-element models of the conductive filament's temperature field, and how interface thermal properties set the peak.

What this work is about

Resistive switching is a thermal event as much as an electrical one: the filament's temperature during SET and RESET governs ion migration, retention, and variability. This work builds coupled electrothermal finite-element models of TaOx filaments in COMSOL Multiphysics, calibrating thermal boundary conductance at the electrode interfaces and extracting interface and vertical-cutline temperature profiles across parametric studies of bias, filament geometry, and composition.

Why it matters

Heat doesn't respect cell boundaries. In a dense crossbar array, thermal energy generated in one filament doesn't stay contained — it conducts outward through the oxide stack, bleeds into neighboring electrodes, and can silently disturb the resistance state of adjacent, unselected cells. This is thermal crosstalk, and it's one of the most insidious scalability bottlenecks facing ReRAM as arrays shrink and densities climb.

Analog In-Memory Computing

Matrix-vector multiplication in the memory array itself — and what device nonidealities cost you at the system level.

What this work is about

A ReRAM crossbar computes matrix-vector products in one shot: weights live as conductances, inputs arrive as voltages, and Kirchhoff's law sums the products on each column. The catch is that real devices are noisy, drift, and hold fewer reliable levels than the math wants. This work develops multi-level conductance targeting with write-verify programming, and bit-slice architectures that spread a weight's bits across several devices so each one only needs modest precision.

From device to system

Using Sandia's CrossSim framework, measured device statistics propagate up to network-level accuracy, closing the loop between programming strategy and application performance. The motivating applications are energy-constrained on both ends of the spectrum — edge inference on space platforms, where radiation tolerance and power budgets collide, and data centers where energy has become the binding constraint on AI workloads.

FinFET Device Characterization

Extracting the numbers that define a technology — subthreshold slope, threshold voltage, and effective drive current for every device flavor in the ASAP7 PDK.

My Work

Characterized all four ASAP7 FinFET Devices : SLVT, LVT, RVT, and SRAM, NMOS and PMOS — in HSPICE across −40 °C, 25 °C, and 120 °C. Extracted subthreshold swing and constant-current threshold voltage with scripted .measure commands, validated against hand calculations from waveform in WaveViewer, and measured effective drive current (IEFF) using the dual-transistor IH/IL method. Computed fin counts from effective-width geometry (Weff = N·(2hfin + wfin)).

Demonstration

Fluency in device-level SPICE methodology: measurement automation rather than cursor-reading, cross-checking simulated extractions against first-principles calculations, and connecting VT/SS/leakage trade-offs to real design choices like why SLVT burns leakage for speed on critical paths while RVT carries the bulk of a design.
