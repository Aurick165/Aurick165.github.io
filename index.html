Arizona State University

Two problems wear the same face in my research: making AI accelerators efficient enough to run at the edge of a satellite, and making them survive the environment they'd be flying through. My Ph.D. work at Arizona State University, in Dr. Matthew Marinella's group, lives at that intersection — radiation-hardened analog AI accelerators built on resistive memory, for space platforms and for data centers where energy has become the binding constraint.

What I bring to it is a device-physics foundation and carried forward: TCAD modeling of compound-semiconductor and advanced-node devices, ML-driven optimization for inverse device design, and radiation-effects analysis that runs from TCAD through circuit simulation. Different devices and circuits, same instinct: Understand the physics and math first, then decide what are allowed to abstract away.

III-Nitride Electronic Devices — ML-driven design

Combined NSGA-II multi-objective optimization with neural networks over 472 TCAD-simulated AlGaN/GaN HEMT structures to maximize breakdown voltage while minimizing gate leakage and current collapse; parameter-variation studies in Sentaurus in collaboration with an international semiconductor foundry, plus ML-based compact modeling and field-plate studies for RF devices.

2023 – 2025 · BUET

Silicon Power Devices — LDMOS & SOI-LDMOS

TCAD thermal analysis of junction temperature and leakage in LDMOS vs. SOI-LDMOS, Baliga figure-of-merit benchmarking of Ron,sp against breakdown voltage, COMSOL verification of thermal RC against analytical models, and rapid inverse design of field-plate LDMOS for BV / Ron,sp co-optimization.

2024 – 2025

Neural Networks for TCAD Acceleration

cGAN-based augmentation of Sentaurus TCAD image datasets and stable-diffusion generation of TDR images for AlGaN/GaN HEMTs, bypassing expensive physics simulations for device design loops.

2024 – 2025

Radiation Effects in 3 nm CFET
